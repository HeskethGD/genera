Algebraic curves
----------------

The algebraic-curve module provides numerical computations with supported
smooth plane curves. It computes branch loci, monodromy, genus, homology,
period matrices, Riemann constants, Abel maps, and integrals. The results are
used with the higher-genus theta and Kleinian functions documented in
:doc:`abelian`.

Historically, a genus-:math:`g` curve has :math:`g` linearly independent
holomorphic differentials. Their periods determine a :math:`g`-dimensional
Jacobian, useful in algebraic geometry and integrable systems.

Conventions
...........

**Baker basis.** For supported hyperelliptic curves, the automatic first-kind
basis consists of monomials :math:`x^k dx/z`. For a general curve, automatic
holomorphic forms are selected from interior Newton-polygon points when the
edge and genus checks pass. Otherwise the caller supplies a basis.

**BEL conventions.** The automatic hyperelliptic second-kind basis and
half-period signs follow [BEL1997]_. The returned ``kappa`` is compatible with
the first-kind marking and the Kleinian functions.

**Period matrices.** Full first-kind matrices are :math:`2\omega` and
:math:`2\omega'`. The normalized Riemann matrix is
:math:`\tau=\omega^{-1}\omega'`. The complete period lattice is generated by
the columns of :math:`2\omega` and :math:`2\omega'`.

**Riemann constants.** Genera uses the additive convention
:math:`\theta(A(D)+K,\tau)=0`, with characteristic representation
:math:`K=\tau a+b` modulo the lattice generated by ``[I, tau]``.

The Curve class
...............

``Curve`` is the unifying object for topology, periods, integration, and Abel
maps. Construct a curve from a sparse mapping, an ascending coefficient
sequence for :math:`y^2=P(x)`, or an explicit numerical context::

   >>> from genera import algebraic_curve
   >>> curve = algebraic_curve({(0, 2): 1, (1, 0): 1, (3, 0): -1})
   >>> curve.genus
   1

The polynomial must depend on :math:`y`; a polynomial independent of :math:`y`
is rejected. Expensive stages are lazy and cached by precision. If the
numerical context changes after construction, relevant stages are recomputed.

Computational engines
.....................

The hyperelliptic engine handles curves of the form
:math:`A y^2+B(x)y+C(x)=0` with constant nonzero :math:`A`, provided the
transformed polynomial has distinct roots. The change
:math:`z=y+B(x)/(2A)` gives
:math:`z^2=B(x)^2/(4A^2)-C(x)/A`. Its automatic forms are
:math:`x^k dx/z`, and its period marking is deterministic.

The geometric polygon engine handles non-hyperelliptic curves and calculations
with caller-supplied differentials. Without supplied forms it can select
:math:`x^{a-1}y^{b-1}dx/F_y` from interior Newton-polygon points. Supplying
one callable per genus selects this engine, even for a hyperelliptic model.

The ``engine`` and ``marking`` fields on period records identify the convention
used. Results from different markings must not be combined without an
integral symplectic cycle transformation.

Places, paths, and integration
...............................

Places over a regular finite value are labelled by ``fibre``. A ``CurvePath``
tracks a lifted path and can be passed to ``integral`` with one differential or
a sequence of differentials.
